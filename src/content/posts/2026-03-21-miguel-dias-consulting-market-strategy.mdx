## 1. Current reality tree

![Current Reality Tree v2](/images/2026-03-21/crt-v2.png)

**Key causal chains not obvious from the diagram:**

- UDE-16 (AI creates WIP) is the engine that drives most of the tree. It feeds upward into: leaders drowning (UDE-18), AI spend having no ROI (UDE-17), companies not fixing delivery (UDE-15), and Kanban being hollowed out (UDE-19). The Faros AI report confirms this: teams with high AI adoption produce +21% tasks and +98% PRs, but review time increases 91% and at company level there is zero correlation between AI adoption and throughput/DORA improvements.
- UDE-15 (companies fail at delivery but don't fix it) is the most frustrating node. You've seen departments fail spectacularly and get shut down, yet companies still don't invest in fixing the underlying flow problem. This connects to the deep root cause: leaders don't perceive delivery as a solvable problem — they see it as inherent complexity.
- The German market saturation signal (UDE-20) is that DevOps roles requiring German language skills indicates enough local candidates exist. Meanwhile, 26.2% fewer IT positions were posted year-over-year (16,500 fewer in absolute terms).

## 2. Evaporating cloud

![Evaporating Cloud](/images/2026-03-21/evaporating-cloud.png)

**The injection in plain language:** stop selling VSM. Stop selling "faster delivery." Instead, sell the answer to the AI productivity paradox — "your AI tools are creating more work-in-progress but your delivery isn't getting faster, and I can show you where the bottleneck is and fix it." VSM and flow management are how you deliver this. But the buyer never hears the term unless they want to.

This lets both sides of the cloud coexist: you stay true to what you know (B), while speaking the language the market uses (C).

**This injection is unvalidated.** The experiments below are designed to test it.

## 3. Demand research plan

### Objective

Find out what the market is actually buying — not what we think they should buy.

### Data sources

| Source | What it gives us | Time estimate |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| **freelancermap.com** | Active project listings in DACH: descriptions, skills, rates, duration, German requirement, remote/on-site | 2-3 hours |
| **LinkedIn Jobs** | Permanent + contract roles in Berlin/Germany: skills, salary, company type, job description language | 2-3 hours |
| **XING** | German-market-specific demand, Mittelstand companies, German-language roles | 1-2 hours |
| **Berlin Startup Jobs** | Startup-specific demand, less likely to require German, more AI-native problems | 1 hour |
| **GULP, Malt, tribeworks** (lower priority) | Enterprise gigs (GULP), consulting marketplace (Malt), AI-matched projects (tribeworks) | 1-2 hours |

### Search terms to run on each platform

Record for each: **# results, sample titles (3), rate range, German required (y/n), remote (y/n), typical duration.**

**Your current positioning:**
- "value stream" / "value stream mapping"
- "agile coach"

**Adjacent categories:**
- "Scrum Master"
- "process improvement" / "Prozessoptimierung"
- "engineering effectiveness" / "developer productivity"
- "DORA metrics" / "engineering metrics"

**Your technical background:**
- "DevOps"
- "cloud infrastructure" / "Kubernetes" / "Terraform"
- "platform engineering"

**The AI angle (testing the injection):**
- "AI adoption" / "KI-Einführung"
- "AI implementation"
- "AI" + "software delivery"

**Employment-specific:**
- "engineering manager" Berlin
- "VP engineering" / "head of engineering" Berlin
- "fractional" / "part-time" + any of the above

### Research outputs to compile

1. **Demand heat map** — search term × platform × number of results
2. **Language analysis** — actual words buyers use in descriptions, grouped by theme
3. **Rate benchmarking** — what each category pays (baseline: management consulting €120/hr, AI/ML +20-30% premium)
4. **German requirement rate** — % of roles requiring German, by category
5. **Gap analysis** — high demand + your skills fit + lower competition

## 4. Validated learning experiments

Each experiment is: hypothesis → test → metric → minimum success criteria → time box → connection to the CRT/cloud.

### Experiment 1: Demand mapping

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | There are active, paying gigs on German freelance platforms that match my skills (DevOps + process improvement + flow) even if they don't mention VSM. |
| **Test** | Complete the demand research plan above. |
| **Metric** | Number of active listings I could credibly apply to, grouped by category. |
| **Min. success** | ≥20 active listings across platforms that I'm qualified for. |
| **Time box** | 1 week — complete by March 27 (before the trip). |
| **CRT connection** | Directly addresses the **core problem** ("I don't know what the market is willing to buy"). If ≥20 listings found → the problem is positioning, not demand. If fewer than 20 → market contraction is severe, prioritize employment. |

### Experiment 2: Reframed LinkedIn content

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Content framed around "the AI productivity paradox" gets significantly more engagement than content framed around VSM. |
| **Test** | Publish two posts same week. Post A: standard VSM insight. Post B: same insight reframed around AI creating WIP + bottlenecks absorbing gains (using Faros AI data as hook). |
| **Metric** | Impressions, engagement rate, and DMs/comments from leaders (director+). |
| **Min. success** | Post B gets 2x engagement of Post A AND ≥1 DM or comment from a leader. |
| **Time box** | Publish by March 24. Measure by April 7. |
| **CRT connection** | Tests the **evaporating cloud injection** — does leading with AI-induced pain resonate? Addresses UDE-6 (messaging doesn't land) and UDE-14 (undifferentiated pitch). |

### Experiment 3: Outreach sprint to network

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Targeted outreach framed around a specific pain point (not selling VSM) generates discovery calls. |
| **Test** | Message 50 engineering leaders (directors, VPs, Heads of Eng) with: "I've been talking to leaders who say AI tools aren't translating into faster delivery — is that something you're seeing too?" No selling. Just a conversation. |
| **Metric** | Response rate and calls booked. |
| **Min. success** | 10% response rate (5 responses) + ≥2 discovery calls. |
| **Time box** | Send all 50 within 1 week. Allow 2 weeks for responses. Complete by April 14. |
| **CRT connection** | Tests whether the problem is outreach **volume** (UDE-5) or **messaging** (UDE-6) or both. Also generates qualitative data about what leaders actually experience — feeds back into the CRT and validates/invalidates the WIP hypothesis (UDE-16). |

### Experiment 4: Freelance platform applications

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | I can get responses from freelance platforms if I position around DevOps/platform/process improvement rather than VSM. |
| **Test** | Apply to 10 relevant projects on freelancermap (identified in Experiment 1) with reframed profile and pitch. |
| **Metric** | Response rate and interview invitations. |
| **Min. success** | ≥2 responses out of 10 applications. |
| **Time box** | Applications within 1 week of completing Exp. 1. Allow 2 weeks for responses. |
| **CRT connection** | Tests whether the **cloud injection** works in practice. Also tests the German language barrier (UDE-20) — if all interesting listings require German, that's critical data. |

### Experiment 5: Employment market test

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Part-time (60-80%) engineering roles exist in Berlin that don't require German and allow continued Miguel Dias Consulting work. |
| **Test** | Search LinkedIn Jobs + Berlin Startup Jobs for part-time/contract/fractional roles. Apply to 5. |
| **Metric** | Qualifying roles found + response rate. |
| **Min. success** | ≥5 roles found that are part-time and don't require German. ≥1 interview from 5 applications. |
| **Time box** | Search during Exp. 1. Apply within 1 week after. Results within 3 weeks. |
| **CRT connection** | Addresses UDE-2 (job search splitting focus). Tests whether job search and business development use the same muscle — both require outreach, offering, and market understanding. |

## 6. Key market data collected so far

**German freelance market (2025-2026):** Overall market slowed 20%+ since 2023. AI/ML demand nearly quadrupled in the same period. Top-paid: SAP (€111/hr), Management/IT Consulting (€120/hr). Highest-growth skills: Python + AI libraries, Cloud Security, Data Engineering, Compliance (GDPR/DORA). 56% of freelancers now acquire work through networks (up from 30%). Project acquisition is the #1 challenge for 60% of freelancers.

**The AI productivity paradox (Faros AI):** Teams with high AI adoption: +21% tasks, +98% PRs merged. But: +91% review time, +9% bugs, +154% PR size. At company level: zero correlation between AI adoption and throughput/DORA/quality. Root cause: downstream bottlenecks absorb all gains.

**AI adoption in Germany (empirical study, Jan 2026):** Experience level moderates benefits. Biggest barrier: limited awareness of project context. German orgs face additional GDPR + EU AI Act pressures. Mittelstand lacks resources for private AI infrastructure. Over 80% of firms across US/UK/Germany/Australia report no impact on productivity from AI despite 70% actively using it.
