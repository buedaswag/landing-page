## 2. Evaporating cloud

![Evaporating Cloud v2](/images/2026-03-21/evaporating-cloud-v2.png)

**The injection in plain language:** stop selling VSM. Stop selling "faster delivery." Instead, sell the answer to the AI productivity paradox — "your AI tools are creating more work-in-progress but your delivery isn't getting faster, and I can show you where the bottleneck is and fix it." VSM and flow management are how you deliver this. But the buyer never hears the term unless they want to.

This lets both sides of the cloud coexist: you stay true to what you know (B), while speaking the language the market uses (C).

**This injection is unvalidated.** The experiments below are designed to test it.

**Key point:** All three injection options (repackage, pivot, hybrid) require the same first step — find out what people are actually spending money on. The demand research and experiments resolve the uncertainty blocking all paths.

**What would overcome the buyer's UDEs?** This is what to listen for in discovery calls:
- B-1 (expensive/risky): What if the offering is low-commitment? A diagnostic, not a retainer.
- B-2 (burned before): What if there's a money-back guarantee or pay-for-outcomes model?
- B-3 (why not AI): What if the pitch is "make your AI investment actually work" not "replace AI with me"?
- B-4 (no bandwidth): What if onboarding is near-zero? Self-serve assessment or async delivery?
- B-5 (context): What if the first step is mapping their context, which is inherently valuable by itself?
- B-6 (part-time): What if part-time is framed as "fractional" (like fractional CTO) — a recognized model?
- B-7 (cost > pain): What if the pain gets worse? AI adoption is accelerating. The dysfunction DORA documents will intensify. The question is whether leaders feel it enough to act.

## 3. Customer's current reality tree

![Customer CRT](/images/2026-03-21/customer-crt.png)

**This is the missing piece from the Goldratt approach.** In *It's Not Luck*, the characters don't just map their own UDEs — they map the customer's UDEs and build the unrefusable offer around the customer's pain, not their own expertise.

**Key insight:** The customer's evaporating cloud is: "I need to improve delivery (the pain is real) BUT I can't risk budget/reputation on something unproven." So they default to doing nothing, asking AI, or buying a tool — all lower-risk, lower-commitment options. Your real competition isn't other VSM consultants. It's inaction and free alternatives.

**Goldratt's value levers applied to Miguel Dias Consulting:**
- **Reduce lead time to value:** Can you show results in 2 weeks instead of 3 months? A quick diagnostic that surfaces their top bottleneck and gives them one actionable fix immediately.
- **Reduce inventory/risk:** Minimize upfront commitment. No big contract. Diagnostic first. Pay-for-outcomes if possible.
- **Increase throughput/value:** Tie your impact to metrics they already track (DORA metrics, OKRs, deployment frequency). Quantify the cost of their current state: "what does 1 month of delivery delay cost you?"
- **Reduce operating expense:** Frame your fee against the cost of the problem, not against other consultants.

**All customer UDEs and risks are hypothesized.** They need validation through Experiment 3 (outreach + discovery calls). The open questions at the bottom of the diagram are the agenda for those calls.

## 4. Where is the bottleneck in my funnel?

My business funnel: **Visibility → Trust → Trigger → Offer → Conversion → Delivery → Referrals**

There's evidence that the early stages are working. People spontaneously show up for workshops. Collaborations happen. Some business deals have closed. LinkedIn following is growing (4,000+ followers, 280+ engineering leaders). Trust is building. But it's not converting to revenue. So the bottleneck is somewhere between trust and conversion.

**Hypotheses for where the constraint sits:**

| Bottleneck hypothesis | Evidence for | Evidence against | How to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trust exists but with wrong people** — peers and practitioners, not budget holders | Workshop attendees are mostly practitioners, not VPs/directors | Some leaders do engage (BeeHealthy, collaborators) | Check: of my 280+ engineering leaders, how many are director+ with budget authority? |
| **Trust exists but no trigger to buy** — they like me but never think "I need Miguel NOW" | No urgency mechanism in my offering. VSM is "nice to have." | — | Experiment 2: does framing around AI crisis create urgency? Experiment 3: do leaders respond to a pain-point question? |
| **Trust exists but offer isn't clear** — they don't connect their pain to what I do | "VSM" doesn't map to any pain they'd describe. "Faster delivery" is generic. | People who did engage understood the value after the workshop | Experiment 2: test different framing. Experiment 3: ask what they'd search for. |
| **Trust exists but the ask is too big** — jumping from "attended a workshop" to "€10K+ engagement" | No intermediate step between free content and paid consulting | BeeHealthy started with a scoped offsite, not a retainer | Design a low-commitment entry point: diagnostic, assessment, half-day session |

**The most likely answer:** it's a combination — mostly wrong audience (practitioners not buyers) + no trigger + too big a jump from trust to purchase. The demand research and outreach experiments will clarify which one dominates.

**If marketing is the bottleneck** (not enough people know about me at all), then the 1,000-person outreach weeks and LinkedIn content experiments address it directly. **If it's conversion** (people know me but don't buy), then the customer CRT and unrefusable offer design are the fix. The experiments are designed to distinguish between these.

## 5. Demand research plan

### Objective

Find out what the market is actually spending money on.

### Data sources

| Source | What it gives us | Time |
|--------|-----------------|------|
| **freelancermap.com** | Active project listings DACH: skills, rates, duration, German req, remote | 2-3 hrs |
| **LinkedIn Jobs** | Permanent + contract roles Berlin/Germany: skills, salary, company type, language | 2-3 hrs |
| **XING** | German-market demand, Mittelstand, German-language roles | 1-2 hrs |
| **Berlin Startup Jobs** | Startup demand, less likely to require German | 1 hr |
| **GULP, Malt, tribeworks** | Enterprise gigs, consulting marketplace, AI-matched projects | 1-2 hrs |

### Search terms (run on each platform, record: # results, sample titles, rate range, German req, remote, duration)

**Current positioning:** "value stream", "agile coach"

**Adjacent:** "Scrum Master", "process improvement" / "Prozessoptimierung", "engineering effectiveness", "DORA metrics"

**Technical background:** "DevOps", "Kubernetes" / "Terraform", "platform engineering"

**AI angle:** "AI adoption" / "KI-Einführung", "AI implementation"

**Employment:** "engineering manager" / "VP engineering" / "head of engineering" Berlin, "fractional" / "part-time"

### Outputs

1. Demand heat map (search term × platform × results count)
2. Language analysis (actual buyer words from descriptions)
3. Rate benchmarking by category
4. German requirement rate by category
5. Gap analysis (high demand + skills fit + lower competition)

## 8. Key market data

**German freelance market (2025-2026):** Market slowed 20%+ since 2023. AI/ML demand nearly quadrupled. Top-paid: SAP €111/hr, Management/IT Consulting €120/hr. 56% acquire work through networks. Project acquisition is #1 challenge for 60%.

**DORA 2025 (n=4,916):** 90% use AI. 80%+ say it increased productivity. 30% don't trust AI-generated code. AI improves throughput BUT increases instability. Zero effect on burnout/friction. Higher expectations absorb capacity gains. "Without VSM, AI creates localized pockets of productivity lost to downstream chaos." VSM is "force multiplier for AI." 7 team archetypes; 40% are high performers.

**Faros AI:** +21% tasks, +98% PRs, +91% review time, +9% bugs, +154% PR size. Zero company-level correlation with throughput/DORA/quality.

**AI adoption in Germany (Jan 2026 study):** #1 barrier is limited awareness of project context. GDPR + EU AI Act pressures. 80% of firms report no productivity impact despite 70% using AI.
