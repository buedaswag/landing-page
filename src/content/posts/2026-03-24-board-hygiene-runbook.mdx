**Flow:** Inbox → Ready (cap: 15–20) → Committed (cap: 10–12) → WIP (limit: 3) → Done → Archive

**Sizing:** S (under 1 hr) · M (1–4 hr) · L (half day+) · XL (break it down)

**Priority:** Delivery > Search (value × prob.) > Brand (timeboxed)

**Bets:** 3–6 active · every Ready card tags to a bet · review monthly

**Cadence:** 20 min weekly grooming · monthly relevance check

## ◧ Board Structure

*Same board, two lenses*

**Two labels: Ready and Committed**

"Ready" = passes the 4-sentence DoR test, but you haven't decided to do it yet. It's the pool of things you *could* pull.

"Committed" = you've decided this is happening — it's in your active working set for this week/period.

Cards without either label are ideas. Filter by "Committed" for your working view. Filter by "Ready" to see your options during grooming.

**Columns = flow stages, not work categories**

Restructure columns to: Inbox → Ready → Committed → In Progress (WIP=3) → Waiting/Blocked → Done. Use Trello labels for category (Outreach, Learning, Client Work, Website, etc.) instead of dedicated columns.

**Pool caps**

Ready pool: up to 15–20 cards. These are your "shelf" of well-defined options.

Committed pool: no more than 10–12 cards. These are the things you've decided to do.

When you finish a WIP item, pull from Committed. When Committed runs low, promote from Ready during grooming.

**Freezer label**

Cards in the idea pool with no due date and no activity for 4+ weeks get a "Frozen" label (or move to a Freezer column at the far left). Not deleted — just out of your default view.

## ◈ Bets & Value

*Connect tasks to revenue*

**What's a bet?**

A bet is a small, active initiative with a clear hypothesis about how it generates money. Keep 3–6 active bets at any time. Every task on your board should tag to one. Tasks that don't connect to any bet are either maintenance (timebox it) or ideas without a clear revenue path (keep in idea pool, don't promote to Ready).

**Bet labels in Trello**

Use a separate label color for bets, with a naming convention like "BET: [name]". Examples:

- BET: BeeHealthy engagement
- BET: March online cohort
- BET: Conference pipeline
- BET: Content engine

Review active bets monthly. Retire closed/dead ones, add new ones.

**Rough value sizing per bet**

Each bet gets a rough expected value:

- Deal size × probability of closing
- E.g., €5K engagement at 60% = €3K expected
- E.g., €50 × 10 seats at 80% = €400 expected

You don't need precision — just enough to compare bets against each other and avoid spending a week on a €200 bet when a €5K bet needs attention.

**Prioritization logic**

Your existing framework + bets:

1. Delivery first — active client commitments always win
2. Among "Search" bets — highest expected value × probability
3. "Build Brand" bets get protected timebox hours, not priority queue position. They're the long game — don't starve them, but don't let them crowd out rent-paying work

During weekly grooming, check: what's the distribution of Ready/WIP cards across bets? Are you focused or scattered?

**Your revenue funnel (reference)**

Visibility → Leads → Discovery calls → Proposals → Closed deals → Delivery → Testimonials/Referrals → (loops back)

Each bet lives somewhere in this funnel. Bets closer to "Closed deals" have higher short-term value. Bets at the "Visibility" end are investments. Balance both, but know which is which.

## ✓ Definition of Ready

*Gate before commitment*

**The 4-sentence test**

A card is Ready when you can say:

1. "The next concrete action is: [one sentence]"
2. "This is a size [S / M / L]"
3. "Done looks like: [one sentence]"
4. "This serves bet: [bet name]" (or "maintenance")

**T-shirt sizing**

- S = under 1 hour
- M = 1–4 hours
- L = half day or more
- XL = needs to be broken down before it's Ready

**Track actual vs. estimated**

Use a custom field for estimated size (S/M/L) and note actual time spent. Over time, this data lets you calibrate your estimates and eventually feed Monte Carlo simulations.

## ◉ Definition of Done

*Prevent scope creep*

**Done means done**

The card's "done looks like" statement from the DoR is satisfied. No gold-plating. If new scope emerges during execution, capture it as a new card in the Inbox — don't expand the current one.

**Move and timestamp**

When done, move the card to Done column. Record the completion date. This is your cycle time data. Archive Done cards weekly so the column stays clean.

## ⏱ Weekly Grooming Ritual

*20–25 min · same day each week*

**Step 1 — Scan for urgency (3 min)**

Look at cards with upcoming due dates across the whole board. Anything that needs to move to Ready or WIP this week? Time-sensitive future items (e.g., "applications open in September") stay in the pool with their date — don't freeze these.

**Step 2 — Freeze the stale (3 min)**

Any card in the idea pool with no due date and no activity for 4+ weeks → Frozen label. Don't agonize. If it matters, it will resurface.

**Step 3 — Promote Ideas → Ready (5 min)**

If Ready pool is getting thin, pick items from the idea pool. Apply the Definition of Ready (4-sentence test + sizing). If it doesn't pass, leave it or refine it.

**Step 4 — Promote Ready → Committed (4 min)**

If Committed pool is below 8 cards, pull the highest-value Ready cards. Use your priority logic: Delivery > Search (highest expected value × probability) > Brand. Check bet distribution — are you focused or scattered?

**Step 5 — Check WIP & blocked (3 min)**

Are you at or below WIP limit of 3? Is anything blocked? Move blocked cards to the Blocked column to free WIP slots. Anything lingering longer than expected? Decision: push through, ask for help, or kill it.

**Step 6 — Archive Done (2 min)**

Move completed cards from Done to archive. Quick scan: does the data on size vs. actual time look right? Note any big misses for calibration.

**Step 7 — Calendar sync (2 min)**

Check that your recurring capacity blocks (see below) are still on the calendar for next week. Adjust if needed based on what's in WIP and Ready.

## ▦ Capacity Reservations

*Protect time, not tasks*

**Recurring calendar blocks**

These aren't for specific tasks — they reserve capacity by work type:

- Learning: ~2 hrs/week
- Outreach: ~3 hrs/week
- Maintenance / compliance: ~1 hr/week
- Board grooming: 20 min/week

Adjust ratios based on current business priorities.

**How to use them**

When you pull a card into WIP, you're drawing from the capacity you've already allocated for that category. If your outreach Ready queue is full but your calendar has no outreach block this week, either add one or acknowledge it won't happen this week.

## ⇥ Exit Policy

*How cards leave the board*

**4-week freeze rule**

Idea pool cards with no due date and no activity for 4 weeks → Frozen. Reviewed quarterly, bulk-archived if still untouched.

**2-week blocked rule**

Cards in Waiting/Blocked for more than 2 weeks get a decision: escalate, redefine, or kill.

**Monthly relevance check**

Once a month, ask for every card on the board: "If I weren't already doing this, would I start it today?" If no → archive or freeze.

**Weekly Done archival**

Archive Done cards every week during grooming. Keep the board showing only active and upcoming work.

## ◔ Path to Monte Carlo

*Build the data habit now*

**Phase 1 — Estimate and record (now)**

Add T-shirt size (S/M/L) to every card when it enters Ready. Track when cards enter WIP and when they reach Done. Use Trello custom fields or the card age power-up you already have.

**Phase 2 — Calibrate (after ~6 weeks)**

Review: How long do S/M/L cards actually take? Are your estimates consistently off in one direction? Adjust the size definitions or your expectations.

**Phase 3 — Right-sizing (after ~3 months)**

Look at which size bucket has the most predictable throughput. Aim to break work into that size. This is your "right-sizing" — optimizing for flow, not just estimation accuracy.

**Phase 4 — Simulation (when ready)**

With enough cycle time data per size category, you can run Monte Carlo forecasting: "Given my throughput of the last 8 weeks, how many items can I likely finish in the next 2 weeks?" Tools like ActionableAgile or even a spreadsheet can do this.
