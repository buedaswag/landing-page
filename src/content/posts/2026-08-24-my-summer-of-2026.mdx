## About my great-grandfather

<img src="/images/2026-summer/image-28.png" alt="José Alves Monteiro" width="450" />

**José Alves Monteiro** (1890–1980), born in **Fundão** to Ana Tavares Monteiro and the local small-scale farmer José Alves Monteiro, was a distinguished Portuguese magistrate and intellectual who left a cultural and civic mark on his home region. After graduating in Law from the University of Coimbra in 1912, he returned to Fundão to practice law and engage in local culture and politics. During this early period, he collaborated with the Integralist movement. This influential political and cultural current, known as *Integralismo Lusitano*, emerged in Coimbra in 1914 as a traditionalist, anti-liberal, and anti-parliamentary movement that championed municipal decentralization, Roman Catholicism, and a corporatist monarchy. Around the same time, he provided critical support to what would become the **Seminário Menor do Fundão** (Minor Seminary), serving dedicatedly as an unpaid volunteer teacher there for several years. Alongside a brilliant legal career — during which he restructured the Criminal Investigation Police into the modern *Polícia Judiciária* and ascended to the Supreme Court of Justice — he remained a pioneering local archaeologist, founding and organizing the **Archaeological Museum of Fundão** in 1942. He also published literary works, such as the poetry collection *Terras da Beira*. Throughout his life, Monteiro was widely remembered as a generous citizen who used his professional network and personal influence to help any local citizens of Fundão who sought his assistance.
